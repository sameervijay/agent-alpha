Expert Call: Former VP of Product at Cadence Design Systems
Date: February 14, 2026
Duration: 45 minutes

EXPERT BIO:
Former VP of Product at Cadence Design Systems (2019-2025), responsible for AI-driven EDA product strategy and go-to-market execution. Led the development and launch of Cadence's AI Super Agent platform. Deep domain expertise in EDA market dynamics, customer adoption patterns, and competitive positioning versus Synopsys.

TRANSCRIPT:

Analyst: Thank you for joining us today. Can you give us your perspective on Cadence's recent AI product launches and how they're being received by customers?

Expert: Absolutely. The ChipStack AI Super Agent that we launched last quarter is seeing significantly stronger customer adoption than we initially projected. When I left Cadence in late 2025, we were tracking about 15 design wins in the pipeline. I've stayed in touch with some folks still there, and from what I understand, they're now at over 40 active customer engagements, with several tier-1 hyperscalers committing to multi-year licenses.

The key differentiator is that ChipStack isn't just an incremental improvement - it's fundamentally changing the design cycle time. We're seeing customers report 25-30% reductions in time-to-tapeout for complex AI chips. That's a game-changer because it means they can iterate faster and get to market ahead of competitors.

Analyst: That's significantly better than the industry averages we've been hearing. What's driving this adoption?

Expert: Three things. First, the AI models underlying ChipStack are trained on Cadence's massive proprietary design database - decades of customer designs. That gives it a pattern recognition advantage that competitors simply can't match. Synopsys is playing catch-up here.

Second, the integration with Cadence's existing Virtuoso and Genus tools is seamless. Customers aren't ripping and replacing - they're adding AI capabilities on top of workflows they already trust. That lowers the barrier to adoption dramatically.

Third - and this is the real kicker - the pricing model. Cadence structured this as a premium add-on rather than a separate product. They're capturing 15-20% price increases on core EDA licenses by bundling AI capabilities. That's pure margin expansion because the incremental cost to deliver is minimal once the AI models are trained.

Analyst: How sustainable is this competitive advantage? Can Synopsys catch up?

Expert: I'd give Cadence at least an 18-24 month lead. Synopsys announced their AI platform, but from what I'm hearing through the grapevine, they're struggling with model training and customer data integration. They don't have the same depth of historical design data that Cadence has accumulated.

More importantly, once a customer commits to ChipStack and retrains their design teams around those workflows, there's significant switching costs. The AI models learn from each customer's specific design patterns over time, so the value compounds. By the time Synopsys has a competitive offering, Cadence will have locked in the top 20-30 customers.

Analyst: What about the IP business - particularly memory interfaces for AI accelerators?

Expert: This is where things get really interesting. Cadence is the only EDA vendor offering all four major memory standards - DDR, LPDDR, GDDR, and HBM. I know from my time there that the memory IP team was working on next-gen HBM3E and HBM4 controllers specifically optimized for AI training workloads.

The hyperscalers designing their own AI chips need these memory interfaces, and they need them to work flawlessly because memory bandwidth is THE bottleneck in AI chip design. Cadence has a captive customer base here. We estimated that by 2027, AI-specific memory IP could represent 30-35% of Cadence's total IP revenue, up from maybe 15% today.

The margin profile on this business is also excellent - once you've designed the IP block, the incremental cost to license it to additional customers is essentially zero. I'd expect to see IP margins expanding 200-300 bps over the next few years as the mix shifts toward these high-value AI components.

Analyst: Any concerns or risks you see?

Expert: The main risk is execution. Cadence is growing fast, and there's always a question of whether they can scale their customer support and engineering teams quickly enough. I've heard some grumbling about response times for support tickets, which could become a problem if not addressed.

The other risk is China. The export controls are a wildcard. Cadence does meaningful business in China, and if that gets cut off entirely, it would hurt. However, I think the secular AI trend more than offsets that risk. The U.S. and European markets alone are big enough to sustain strong growth.

Overall, I'm more bullish on Cadence today than I was when I left. The AI platform is resonating with customers in a way that I honestly didn't expect, and the competitive moat is widening rather than narrowing.

Analyst: Final question - if you had to put a number on it, what kind of revenue growth do you think Cadence can sustain over the next 2-3 years?

Expert: Look, I'm not a financial analyst, but based on what I'm seeing with ChipStack adoption, the AI chip design pipeline, and the memory IP momentum, I'd be surprised if they don't hit mid-to-high teens revenue growth for at least the next 8 quarters. The consensus estimates I've seen look too conservative.

The IP business should grow even faster - low 20s percentage range - because every new AI chip design is a new IP licensing opportunity. And as I mentioned, margins should expand as the mix shifts.

If Cadence executes well, I think they're in a position to significantly outperform consensus over the next couple of years.

END TRANSCRIPT
